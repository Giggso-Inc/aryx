The ingestion journey

Seven checkpoints, one record at a time

Whether it's the 1st record or the 10-millionth, every one takes the same path, which is exactly what keeps the result trustworthy at any scale.

STAGE 01

Extract

Read
InYour database or file→OutOne record, read

Aryx reads records from the connected database or file, one at a time, without altering the original source.

Why it matters — the source system is never touched or put at risk. Aryx only ever works from a copy.

Example — in a real ingested product catalog, one of the first things read is a field called Product, offering choices like Model 6500, Model 6500L, Model 5500, and VX-451, 325 choices in total.

STAGE 02

Land

Store safely
InOne record, read→OutSafely stored copy

Each raw record is saved into Aryx's own storage, along with a note of exactly where it came from.

Why it matters — every piece of information stays traceable back to its original source, permanently.

Example — that Product field, and the business rule tied to it (see Relate below), are both saved exactly as they were read, before anything is interpreted.

STAGE 03

Tag

Understand fields
InSafely stored copy→OutLabeled fields

AI looks at each field and labels what it actually is: an email address, a phone number, an amount of money, and so on.

Why it matters — this runs on inexpensive AI, so understanding your data doesn't get costly as volume grows.

Example — AI recognizes Product as a "choice list" field, pick one from a list, not a free-text field or a number, purely from how it's structured in the source.

STAGE 04

Map

Classify
InLabeled fields→OutProposed record type

Aryx proposes what kind of real-world thing each record represents: a customer, a product, a support ticket, based on its fields.

Why it matters — nothing is assumed silently. Every new classification waits for a person's sign-off first.

Example — Aryx proposes Product represents a "configuration option" on a product catalog, the first time this exact catalog structure is seen, so a person confirms it.

Human checkpoint

"This looks like a Customer, is that right, or is it really an Account?" Aryx pauses and asks whenever it isn't confident.

STAGE 05

Resolve

Find duplicates
InProposed record type→OutMatched or merged record

Aryx compares records to find ones describing the same real-world thing, even if they came from different systems and don't look identical.

Why it matters — clear duplicates merge automatically; anything genuinely uncertain waits for a person, never a guess.

Example — Aryx checks whether a field also called "Product" already exists from a different, previously ingested catalog, and keeps them separate, so choices from one product line never bleed into another.

Human checkpoint

Ambiguous matches wait in a review queue. Every decision made here is saved, so matching keeps improving over time.

STAGE 06

Relate

Connect
InMatched or merged record→OutConnected record

Aryx works out how the now-deduplicated records relate to one another: which customer placed which order, which ticket concerns which product.

Why it matters — this is what turns a pile of clean records into a genuinely connected picture, not just a tidy list.

Example — a real business rule, "Hide UDCC for Non-Privileged Users," gets connected to the two specific settings it's allowed to hide, so the rule and the fields it affects travel together.

STAGE 07

Project

Publish to the graph
InConnected record→OutEntry in the knowledge graph

The finished entities and their relationships are published into the knowledge graph, ready to be explored or asked about.

Why it matters — the graph is always rebuilt from this trusted data, so it can be safely refreshed any time, with nothing lost.

Example — the Product field and the rule that governs it both become connected nodes in the graph, ready for the product-configuration assistant or Ask to use immediately.

The ingestion flow, technically

Ingestion, step by step: where the database and the AI calls happen

The same ingestion journey from earlier, broken into 13 steps, with a single real record traced through every one: an attribute called Product and a rule called Hide UDCC for Non-Privileged Users, both pulled from an actual ingested product catalog (sample.xml). Each step shows exactly what feeds into it and what it hands to the step after.

STEP 01

A job is created

Trigger
InA person starts an ingest, via the web app or the API→OutA tracked job record

Someone points Aryx at a source, a database, or an uploaded file, and confirms they want it ingested. This creates a job the system can track, report on, and resume if interrupted.

Why it matters — nothing starts silently in the background; every ingest is a visible, resumable job from the very first moment.

Example — the job record
job_id8842
sourcesample.xml
workspace_id13
statusqueued
STEP 02

Worker connects to your source

Extract
InQueued job + source location→OutAn open, read-only connection

The background worker picks up the job and opens a connection to your database, or opens an uploaded file directly.

Why it matters — the connection is read-only; Aryx cannot accidentally write back to your source system through it.

STEP 03

Structured files are converted to CSV

Reshape
InThe whole XML (or spreadsheet) file→OutOne CSV per kind of record it detects

If the source is a structured file like XML or a spreadsheet, Aryx first reshapes the whole file into CSV, one CSV per kind of element it detects, before anything else happens. Only then does row-by-row processing begin.

Why it matters — this is the actual step that turns a proprietary export format into rows Aryx already knows how to process, so nothing downstream needs to understand XML specifically.

Example — real conversion output
sample_bm_config_attr.csv427 rows (one per attribute, incl. "Product")
sample_bm_config_rule.csv688 rows (one per rule, incl. "Hide UDCC...")
sample_bm_menu_item.csvone row per selectable value (Model 6500, VX-451, ...)
The single 46,837-line XML file becomes several plain CSV tables, each one a familiar shape Aryx already knows how to ingest.
A different path for messy documents

This conversion only applies to already-structured files. If the source were a PDF or Word document instead, a separate step reads the free text and uses AI to pull out named entities directly, that's where "entity extraction," in the traditional sense, actually happens, and it's a different code path than this one.

STEP 04

One row is streamed

Extract
InA generated CSV (or a live database connection)→OutOne row, read into memory

The worker reads a single row at a time, from the CSV built in the previous step, or directly from a database, never the whole table at once.

Why it matters — this is what makes the exact same process work whether the file has 10 rows or 10 million.

Example — one row from that CSV
id,name,variable_name,menu_type,data_type
39427019,Product,productSelectionProduct_all,1,1
The same "Product" attribute, now a single flat row read from sample_bm_config_attr.csv, one of 427.
STEP 05

Record lands in PostgreSQL

Database write
InOne row→OutA landed_record row

The CSV row from the previous step is written into PostgreSQL immediately, along with a note of exactly where it came from.

Why it matters — this happens before any interpretation, so the original data is always durably captured first, already in the flat shape Aryx works with everywhere else.

Example — what gets written
source_filesample.xml (original upload)
source_tablebm_config_attr, row id=39427019
raw_payloadid=39427019, name=Product, variable_name=productSelectionProduct_all, menu_type=1, data_type=1
The flat row from the generated CSV is stored as-is, still tagged back to the original XML file for provenance, but already in the shape Aryx works with everywhere else, not a nested XML block anymore.
STEP 06

AI Model call: Tag

AI call
Inlanded_record row→OutField-by-field type labels

An AI model reads each column of the landed row and labels what it actually is: an email address, a phone number, an amount of money, a choice list, and so on.

Why it matters — this runs on inexpensive AI, so it scales without getting costly as volume grows.

Example — what the AI labels, column by column
idinternal identifier
namedisplay label ("Product")
variable_nameinternal field code ("productSelectionProduct_all")
menu_type"choice list", pick one from several options
None of this requires knowing anything about CPQ catalogs specifically, the AI is just labeling columns it can see. The 325 actual options don't live in this row anymore, they're their own related file (bm_menu_item.csv), one row per option, each carrying ref_id=39427019 pointing back to this exact record. Relate, later in this flow, is what reconnects them.
STEP 07

Classify: mostly the file name, not AI

Deterministic, plus a narrow AI check
InField-by-field type labels + the file name→OutA proposed entity type, pending approval

For a file with a specific, non-generic name like this one, Aryx trusts the name itself to say what kind of real-world thing the record represents, and only falls back to asking an AI model when a file name is too vague to go on. A different, narrower AI call does run here regardless, not to name the type, but to spot which columns look like matching keys for the Resolve step ahead.

Why it matters — the type itself is decided the same, reliable way every time a clearly-named file comes in; AI is reserved for the one part of this step that genuinely needs judgment.

Example — the proposal
proposed_typebm_config_attr (from the file name, not a guess)
statuspending approval, first time seen on this workspace
STEP 08

PostgreSQL comparison: Resolve

Database read
InProposed entity + its catalog scope→OutA list of candidate matches (or none)

Candidate duplicate or overlapping records already stored in PostgreSQL are looked up and compared against the new one.

Why it matters — this is the step that catches the same field or product showing up from two different sources.

Example — the scoping check
A field code productSelectionProduct_all already exists, but from a different, separately ingested catalog. Aryx keeps them separate using a catalog scope, so this catalog's 325 product choices never mix with the other one's. This same comparison runs for every ingested record, catalog attributes included, not just customer or ticket data.
STEP 09

AI Model call: Adjudicate

AI call
InAn uncertain candidate match→OutA final merge / no-merge decision

For genuinely uncertain matches, an AI model, or a person for the hardest cases, makes the final call. The decision is saved back to PostgreSQL.

Why it matters — every decision made here becomes reusable data for making future matching smarter.

Example — a genuinely uncertain case
A field like Country appears in both of the two ingested catalogs with what looks like the exact same meaning. Is it one shared attribute, or two catalog-specific ones? That call gets made here, and the decision is saved for next time.
STEP 10

Rows are matched by shared ID

Exact match, no AI
InSeparate flat tables sharing a column name→OutAn identified relationship

The CSV conversion at STEP 03 split what used to be one nested XML block into several separate flat tables. This step notices that a column like rule_id in one table exactly matches the id in another, and connects them. No AI is involved, this is deliberately deterministic: exact column-name matches only, never a guess.

Why it matters — without this step, a rule and the fields it actually controls would just be disconnected rows sitting in different tables. Keeping it deterministic means it's never wrong about which rows belong together.

Example — three flat rows, reconnected by a shared ID
bm_config_rule.csvid=47633456, name="Hide UDCC for Non Previliged Users", rule_type=11
bm_config_rule_input.csvrule_id=47633456, attribute_id=59082961, value1=YES
bm_config_marked_attr.csvrule_id=47633456, attribute_id=39426962
bm_config_marked_attr.csvrule_id=47633456, attribute_id=43022701
Four separate rows, from three separate CSVs, all sharing rule_id=47633456. This step connects them into the real rule: unless attribute 59082961 (a privileged-user flag) equals YES, hide attributes 39426962 and 43022701.
A useful, but not fully trusted, shortcut

This connection does get saved, and generically it's eligible to become a graph edge. But for catalog rules specifically, that saved edge isn't always reliable, so later, when the product-configuration assistant actually needs this rule, it re-derives the same connection itself, directly from these same tables, rather than trusting the edge built here.

STEP 11

Relationship is written to PostgreSQL

Database write
InIdentified relationship→OutA stored relationship row

The connection identified in the previous step, the rule, its condition, and the fields it controls, is saved as a linked record.

Why it matters — most parts of Aryx can now look this rule up in one place instead of re-joining the original tables. As the next step notes, the product-configuration assistant specifically chooses not to rely on this and re-derives it directly, as an extra reliability check.

Example — what's saved
rulehideUDCCForNonPreviligedUsers
conditionprivileged-user flag = YES
hidesattribute 39426962, attribute 43022701
STEP 12

Graph nodes & edges are built

Translate
InPostgreSQL rows: entity + relationship→OutGraph-shaped node & edge objects

Aryx translates the durable PostgreSQL rows into the graph's own node-and-edge shape, ready to write.

Why it matters — this translation is what lets the graph be safely thrown away and rebuilt at any time; it is never the original source of the data.

Example — the shapes being built
node(ConfigAttribute: Product)
node(Rule: Hide UDCC for Non-Privileged Users)
edgeProduct —HIDDEN_BY→ Rule
STEP 13

FalkorDB is updated

Graph write
InNode & edge objects→OutA queryable knowledge graph

The finished node and edge objects are written into the knowledge graph, ready to explore or ask about.

Why it matters — the graph is always rebuilt from this trusted data, so it can be safely refreshed any time, nothing is lost.

Example — what lands in the graph
(Configuration Attribute: Product) —HIDDEN_BY→ (Rule: Hide UDCC for Non-Privileged Users) → (Configuration Attribute: the UDCC setting). Now explorable and queryable through Ask. The product-configuration assistant, notably, still re-derives this specific relationship itself from Postgres rather than reading this edge, see the note at "Rows are matched by shared ID" above.
